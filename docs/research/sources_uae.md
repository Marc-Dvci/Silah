# UAE source check

Checked 18 October 2026. The machine-readable version is `sources_uae.json` in this folder.

Method: abstracts and metadata came from Crossref and Europe PMC. Two DoH PDFs and one M42 poster were read as text and images from saved copies. Most UAE government legal sites (uaelegislation.gov.ae, elaws.moj.gov.ae, mohap.gov.ae law pages) refused connections, returned 403, or reset. Nature, Springer and PubMed redirected to login pages. Items marked NOT VERIFIED rest on search summaries or secondary sources.

Headline: the peer-reviewed Emirati carrier paper is first-authored by Jain R, not Chekroun. The Dubai Nature Medicine paper is dated 21 April 2026 and is titled "Citywide premarital genomic screening in a Middle Eastern population".

## 1. Emirati carrier frequencies

Confirmed. Jain R et al., Genet Med Open 2025;3:103446. DOI 10.1016/j.gimo.2025.103446. PMC12397869. Published 19 July 2025. The 2024 Research Square preprint has the same title (DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-5377628/v1, 12 November 2024). Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gimo.2025.103446

- 1,333 Emirati families (346 internal, 987 from literature).
- 1,194 Emirati exomes analysed.
- 701 P/LP variants. 1,060 families had a P/LP variant.
- 52% of variants absent from gnomAD and 30% absent from ClinVar. The abstract says this, and it matches the preprint wording.
- Variant count conflicts. The full-text summary says 854 unique variants. The preprint excerpt says 855. Not resolved.
- Carrier rates: CYP21A2 10.6%; HBB 9.6%; MEFV 5.9%; ABCA4 4.3%; HBA2 1.8%.
- Cumulative allele frequencies in the exome cohort: CYP21A2 5.6%; HBB 5.0%; MEFV 3.0%; ABCA4 2.2%; CFTR 0.34%.
- At-risk couple rate using a provisional gene list: 4% to 21%.

Not found in the text: carrier rates for SMN1, GALC, ARSA, GAA, IDUA, RPE65, HBA1, GJB2, ACADM, BTD, PAH and MMUT. The SMN1 figure is said to come from the 1,502-newborn cohort (section 2), but the value is not in the text. HBA1 deletions were missed by exome sequencing.

Per-gene values were read from a condensed fetch of the PMC page. The per-gene table is in figures or supplements that I did not read. Check them before citing.

M42 and DoH premarital poster, undated: "Premarital screening and genetic counseling in the UAE enabled by the Emirati Genome Program". Source: https://m42.ae/media/irlib1qa/premarital-screening-enabled-by-the-emirati-genome-program.pdf
- More than 1,000 couples screened.
- About 11% positive (11.8% reviewed; 11% positive; 88.2% reported directly negative).
- Random-mating positive rate estimate: 8.5%.
- 570 genes, "over 840 genetic disorders".
- Chart values, which are shares of positive couples and not population carrier frequencies: CYP21A2 14%; MEFV 12%; USH2A 11%; congenital adrenal hyperplasia 15%; familial Mediterranean fever 13%; Usher syndrome 2A 12%; early-onset severe retinal dystrophy 10%; cystic fibrosis 6%.
- The poster's roughly 11% differs from the 565 of 11,748 (about 4.8%) in section 5. Likely a different period or definition.

Not found: per-gene carrier rates for Abu Dhabi premarital screening. Not found: any Emirati Genome Programme paper from 2025 to 2026 with per-gene numbers.

## 2. SMN1 carrier frequency in the UAE, Gulf and Arab populations

Confirmed (UAE). Rabea F et al., Communications Medicine 2024;4:119. Online 15 June 2024. DOI 10.1038/s43856-024-00548-1. Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-024-00548-1
- Newborn screening of 1,502 healthy Emirati newborns.
- SMA carrier frequency 1.3%, reported as 1 in 79 (19 carriers).
- SMA incidence 1 in 7,122 live births. The authors say this is likely an underestimate.
- Clinical cohort: 171 referred patients; 82% Arab from 18 countries; diagnostic yield 33.9%.

Confirmed (Qatar). Ibrahim F et al., Mol Genet Genomic Med 2023;11(12):e2184. DOI 10.1002/mgg3.2184. SMA carrier frequency 2.8% in 13,426 Qatar Biobank samples. Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/mgg3.2184

Not verified (Saudi Arabia). A 2022 study reports a carrier rate of 2.6% in 4,198 volunteers aged 18 to 25. The source page returned 403. The figure is from a search summary only.

Not found: any other Gulf or Arab SMN1 carrier figure, and any newborn-screening result from Abu Dhabi.

## 3. Abu Dhabi newborn screening

Confirmed. DoH launched a genomic newborn screening programme on 18 August 2025. Source: https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-launches-newborn-genetic-screening-programmes
- Whole genome sequencing for "over 815 treatable childhood genetic conditions".
- Named categories: metabolic disorders, immunodeficiencies, haematologic conditions, and "rare diseases such as Spinal Muscular Atrophy".
- SMA is the only specific condition named.
- Results within 21 days.
- Phase one at Kanad Hospital and Danat Al Emarat Hospital, with M42. Expansion to all maternity hospitals is planned, with no date.
- The Media Office copy calls phase one a "voluntary option". The DoH copy does not use the word.

Confirmed. The routine DoH newborn standard is DOH/NBSC/SD/V4/2024, version 4. Publication date January 2025. The effective-date field is blank. Source: https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/5CF37EB2567A4964843F5BC9D83DD156.ashx
- Appendix 7 and Appendix 8 list 40 numbered disorders. These include congenital hypothyroidism, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, sickle cell disease (HbSS, HbSC, HbS/beta-thalassaemia), thalassaemia major, variant haemoglobinopathies, G6PD deficiency, classic PKU, MSUD, tyrosinaemia, homocystinuria, organic acidaemias, fatty acid oxidation disorders, biotinidase deficiency, galactosaemia and cystic fibrosis.
- SMA, Pompe, MPS I, Krabbe and MLD do not appear in the standard text.

Not found: enrolment or results counts for the genomic programme.

## 4. Dubai premarital study in Nature Medicine (s41591-026-04251-7)

Confirmed from the Crossref record and the Europe PMC abstract. Alblooshi K et al. (33 authors, last author Abou Tayoun A). Title: "Citywide premarital genomic screening in a Middle Eastern population". Nature Medicine 2026;32(4):1511-1518. First published online 21 April 2026. PMID 42014881. PMCID PMC13099644. Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04251-7
- 782 genes linked to autosomal recessive disorders, across 18 primary healthcare centres.
- 1,000 couples tested. 79 at risk (8%, 95% CI 6.4 to 9.7).
- Comparison with the Australian Mackenzie's Mission study: 3.9%, P < 0.0001.
- 158 carriers. Four (2.5%, 95% CI 1.0 to 6.3) were homozygous for a pathogenic variant.
- Hemoglobinopathies were the most common concern. 59% of at-risk couples carried variants in 33 other genes.
- 63 of 79 (80%) proceeded with marriage. 16 (20%) did not.

Trade press only: 21 couples with alpha-thalassaemia variants and 11 with beta-thalassaemia or SCD variants (32 couples in total). The government-funded options are the Al Amal Initiative, which covers up to three IVF rounds a year and PGT. Not verified at source.

Conflict: the Illumina page says 570 genes. The paper says 782.

Related but separate: Chekroun I, Rabea F, Jain R, Alsheikh-Ali A, Abou Tayoun A. "Premarital genomic screening in Arab populations of the Middle East". Nature Medicine 31(2), published 9 January 2025. DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03442-4. Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03442-4

## 5. Abu Dhabi premarital totals (Gulf News, 565 incompatible)

Confirmed from the article text. Source: https://gulfnews.com/uae/health/abu-dhabi-genetic-screening-flags-565-incompatible-cases-among-couples-1.500697261
- Author: Abdulla Rasheed, Abu Dhabi editor.
- "11,748 couples underwent genetic testing from the launch of the programme", through July.
- "565 cases of genetic incompatibility were identified, representing around 5 per cent of those tested."
- "570 genes associated with more than 840 autosomal recessive genetic disorders".
- Most common conditions among incompatible couples: congenital adrenal hyperplasia, inherited retinal disorders, primary ciliary dyskinesia, thalassaemia, sickle cell disease, glaucoma. No counts given.
- The publication date is not shown. The page says "Last updated: October 03, 2026".

Funding: not confirmed. The article does not mention funded IVF or PGT. It says counselling covers options such as "genetic testing of embryos and assisted reproductive technologies for eligible cases". The DoH launch release of 25 November 2022 also has no funding statement. Trade press says Abu Dhabi offers funded IVF and PGT to high-risk couples, but that is undated and unverified.

Background: the 2022 DoH release says the pilot started at two centres (Oud Al Touba and Al Maqta) and covers "570 genes for more than 800 medical conditions". Source: https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-to-expand-pre-marital-screening-for-570-genetic-medical-conditions

Not verified: Emirates Health Services reported 3,699 premarital genetic tests in 2025 with 130 incompatible cases. This is from a search summary, and it covers EHS, not DoH Abu Dhabi.

## 6. Emirati Genome Programme

Latest count found: more than 900,000 Emiratis. An Aletihad feature dated 29 July 2026 quotes Dr Mohamed AlAmeri, Director of the Genome and Biobank Division at DoH: "With genetic data from more than 900,000 Emiratis, covering the majority of the national citizen population." Source: https://en.aletihad.ae/news/business/4682076/javasxript:
- Not an official release. The article does not say whether the figure includes non-citizen residents.
- Earlier figure: more than 600,000 genomes at ASHG 2024 (search summary only, not verified).

Results returned to participants: not found. The only example is one anecdote from Arab Health 2025.

Premarital reuse: yes. The 2022 DoH release says the premarital test uses "genetic data provided through the Emirati Genome Programme". The M42 and DoH poster says premarital screening is "enabled by" the programme and uses 30X whole genome sequencing of couples, targeting 570 genes.

## 7. Law

(a) Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 (ICT in health fields), Article 13. Not verified against the official text. Latham and Watkins (19 June 2019), read from a saved copy, says health data "may not be stored, processed, generated, or transferred outside of the UAE, unless such activity has been approved by a decision of the health authority or the Minister." The fine for violating Article 13 is AED 500,000 to 700,000. Article 20 requires 25-year retention. Source: https://lw.com/thoughtLeadership/lw-new-uae-law-regulates-healthcare-data
- Tamimi describes the approver as a health-authority decision in coordination with MOHAP. This differs from Latham.
- MOHAP Resolution No. 51 of 2021 (effective 16 May 2021) is from a search summary only.
- The official text at elaws.moj.gov.ae was unreachable.

(b) Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (PDPL), Article 2(2). Not verified against the official text. The Modulos English-translation page quotes the exclusion as "personal health data that has legislation regulating its protection and processing". So health data is excluded only where its own law applies. Pinsent Masons describes the same carve-out and cites the 2019 health ICT law. That is an inference, not confirmed. Source: https://docs.modulos.ai/frameworks/uae-pdpl/scope-enforcement-and-the-executive-regulation

(c) Federal Law No. 7 of 2019 (medically assisted reproduction). Not verified. A practitioner-guide summary says Article 15 permits preimplantation genetic diagnosis with written spousal consent, to identify hereditary diseases. Sex selection is not confirmed. Federal Decree-Law No. 17 of 2023 amended the law and lets emirates legislate locally. The official text was not reachable.

(d) Termination of pregnancy. Cabinet Resolution No. 44 of 2024, "Concerning the Permitted Abortion Cases", took effect on 21 June 2024. Not verified against the official text. Sources agree on:
- The gestational limit is 120 days (law-firm statements; a Gulf News piece quotes a lawyer).
- Before this, the fetal-anomaly ground was under Article 16 of Federal Decree-Law No. 4 of 2016, with 120 days.
Sources disagree on:
- The new grounds. Gulf News lists non-consensual pregnancy, a perpetrator who is a relative, and a request from both spouses with committee approval. LYLaw lists rape, incest and spousal request. Mondaq lists severe fetal abnormality as a new ground.
- The committee. LYLaw says three doctors, including an obstetrician-gynaecologist. Gulf News says obstetrics and gynaecology, psychiatry, and a public prosecution representative.
Source used: https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/uae-introduces-new-abortion-resolution-heres-what-you-need-to-know-1.1719324525339

(e) Confirmed. "Policy on Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the Healthcare Sector of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi", Department of Health, Ref Policy/AI/0.9, version 0.9, publication date 30 April 2018. Source: https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/E9C1470A575146B18015DEBE57E47F8D.ashx
- A press release is dated 28 May 2018 (search summary).
- The 2025 "Responsible AI Standard" is mentioned in search results but not read.

## 9. Consanguinity

Confirmed (1997). Al Gazali LI et al., Journal of Biosocial Science 29(4):491-497. Source: https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/consanguineous-marriages-in-the-united-arab-emirates/
- Consanguinity 50.5% in the present generation; inbreeding coefficient 0.0222.
- First-cousin marriage 26.2%. The sentence does not say whether this is a share of all marriages or of consanguineous marriages.
- Sample: 2,033 married Emirati women aged 15 and over, in Al Ain and Dubai, October 1994 to March 1995.

Confirmed (2023). Khan GA et al., Journal of Medicine 24(2):82-88 (published 2 July 2023). Source: https://banglajol.info/index.php/JOM/article/view/67269
- About 65% consanguinity. 33% of consanguineous marriages were first cousin or closer.
- Convenience sample from urban areas, collected by students in 2018. Not a national estimate.

Not verified: a Sharjah study (457 adults; 23.4% consanguineous; 14.2% first cousin). Citation and year not found.

Not found: a national estimate from 2024 or 2025.
